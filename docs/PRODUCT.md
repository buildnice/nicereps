# Product contract

## Purpose and boundary

`nicereps` is a free, GPL-3.0 workout tracker for iOS and Android. It is approachable during a workout and configurable for experienced users. It has no community or social feed.

The released app needs no account or backend and makes zero network requests during normal use. Exercise data, media, workouts, photos, and calculations are local. No analytics, ads, subscriptions, in-app purchases, remote images, or remote push. OS-managed device backup, app-store installation, and user-initiated sharing are allowed. With **Health Connect** (Android) or **Apple Health** (iOS) switched on in Settings, finished workouts and body weight are also written to that on-device health store, where apps the user allows can read them, and with **Read body weight** on, body weight other apps saved there is read back; nicereps still makes no network request. EAS network use is confined to development and PR builds; released app updates go through the stores.

## Home and session flow

- One scrolling home page, no tabs. A fixed **Start workout** action opens recent workouts with last-done dates, reusable templates, and **Fresh workout**. The home page shows a 12-week dot heatmap and compact, expandable summaries for muscle sets over the last 7 days, recent workouts, and measurements. Users may reorder or hide summary sections; Start stays first.
- Heatmap brightness reflects the day's completed working-set count: completed normal and drop sets in finished workouts, one per set regardless of muscles. Tapping a day reveals its workouts, exact count, and plan. The heatmap ends 7 days after today, so today and the next 7 days are planned there, and future days look distinct from past ones; there is no separate plan view.
- Starting a workout replaces dashboard content with a scrolling exercise list. Tap an exercise to expand its sets; its ⋮ options hold history and how-to. Below the exercise list, the workout shows its working sets, volume, records and muscles worked. The first time every set is complete, a full-width **Finish** appears at the bottom; once any set is incomplete again it stays hidden until the workout screen is reopened. Tapping Finish with a set not complete scrolls to and expands the first such exercise and marks every incomplete set in the warning colour with a **Not completed** note; a second tap finishes anyway unless a new incomplete set was added since. Switching exercise mid-session is quick; no per-exercise navigation is required.
- A weighted set defaults to weight and reps. A bodyweight lift's weight is the added load; its sets show the body weight under the weight field, and tapping it logs that day's body weight. With no added weight, the weight field shows a greyed BW in place of 0. Optional target RPE, logged/estimated RPE, rest duration, comment, and plates per side on a bar exercise are inline; each set's ⋮ opens a switch per field, and switching one on adds it and focuses its input, Plates aside; the RPE, rest and comment fields show only while that list is open, so a filled value is hidden with them, and a checked plates line stays under the weight. Both RPE values are editable before and after completion. A large tap target completes a set; no completion swipe is required. A normal set warns, without blocking, when its weight is over 1.5× or under half the best completed normal-set weight in the exercise's last 5 finished sessions, or when its reps are at least 3× or at most a third of the most recent completed normal set at that weight. The warning waits until the user leaves that field.
- Which optional fields a set has checked is stored with it. Copying a previous workout retains them. A set's reps from a template, a repeated workout, or the previous set become its target: logged reps start blank with the target shown greyed, and completing a blank set logs the target, and un-completing it blanks it again. A newly added set copies the previous set, or the last session's last set for that exercise: weight, target, optional values, and checked fields, but not completion. Each set shows the completed set at the same position in the last session of that exercise; tapping it copies those values in. When every working set of an exercise met its target reps in its last session, Prev adds a hint of the next weight up (2.5 kg or 5 lb); the set keeps last session's weight, so progression stays optional. Every edit saves immediately; an interrupted session resumes until explicitly finished or discarded.
- Comments can attach to a workout, an exercise within a workout, or a set. An exercise, catalog or custom, also has its own note for setup that carries forward, such as seat height or grip. It shows under the exercise's name in every workout you log, the template editor and its lift page, and can be edited in each of them. It stays apart from the comment on the exercise in one workout. The finished-workout page and its share image leave it out, because they record a single session.
- Deleting a set shows an inline **Set deleted · Undo** in its place for 4 s, or until the next delete while a screen reader is on; **Undo** puts the set back with its values and position. The delete is saved at once, so a set deleted just before the app closes stays deleted.
- Until a finished workout, template, measurement or photo exists, made or imported, home shows a **Getting started** card under Start that explains Start, planning in the 12-week dots, and **Import data**. Erasing all data shows it again.
- A past day's heatmap detail has **Log workout**, which records an empty finished workout on that date, or reuses one with no exercises, and opens it for editing. Closing any finished workout that has no sets deletes it, so a double tap leaves no empty workout. A workout lasting 0 min shows no duration. A finished workout in Recent or day detail opens its own page: duration, working sets, volume, records, muscles worked, and each exercise's top set, recent trend and sets, with **Repeat workout**, **Edit**, **Save as template**, **Share image** and **Delete workout**, which after a second tap deletes the workout and its sets and returns home, where the heatmap, Recent and stats no longer count it. Finishing a workout first opens a check-in: optional 1–5 ratings for **Sleep** (poor to great), **Session felt** (very hard to very easy) and **Energy** (drained to full), and the workout note. Each answer saves as it is given; **Continue** or **Skip** then opens the workout's page, and leaving any other way keeps what was entered. Editing a finished workout never shows it. The workout page shows the ratings as three labelled values, or **Add check-in**, which expands the same choices to change them; the share image leaves them out. They are the `sleep`, `feel` and `energy` columns of `workout.csv`, blank when unset; an older export without them keeps local values, and other apps' imports leave them blank. **Share image** sends a picture of the workout's name, date, duration, working sets, volume and record sets through the system share sheet; nothing leaves the app any other way. **Edit** opens the workout screen, which edits it, moves its date a day at a time up to today, and deletes it after a second tap.

## Exercise and workout model

- One workout may mix **sets/reps**, **duration**, **distance/pace**, **intervals**, and **mark-to-complete** exercises. Duration supports stopwatch and countdown and takes an optional added weight, so one exercise covers a plank with or without a plate. Distance records distance and elapsed time and derives pace. Intervals record work, rest, and rounds. Mark-to-complete needs a tap and optional note.
- Strength sets may be normal, warm-up, or drop sets. Exercises can link into supersets. Tapping a sets/reps set's number cycles it through working, warm-up and drop. Set type, links, RPE, rest, and notes are optional rather than required setup.
- During a workout, completing a set moves on: in a superset it opens the next partner with an unfinished set, and completing an exercise's last set collapses it and opens the next unfinished exercise, wrapping to earlier ones. An exercise with sets left stays open, and when every set is complete all exercises collapse. Editing a finished workout never moves on.
- A bar exercise shows the plates per side for a set's weight in that set's options, using the plates chosen in Settings and the nearest loadable weight when the weight can't be loaded exactly. Bar exercises are catalog barbell, E-Z bar and trap bar exercises and custom exercises whose equipment names one of those, which use the Settings bar, and any sets/reps exercise given its own bar weight in its options; clearing that weight returns it to the Settings bar, or to no bar.
- Completing a set starts its exercise's default rest duration unless that set overrides it. Rest is typed on the keypad; there are no preset rest choices, and a new custom exercise rests 120 s by default. Users can skip or extend the timer. Changing the rest of the set whose rest is running retimes it once the rest field is left, or the app is left while the field is open: the new length counts from the set's completion rather than restarting and replaces any extension, a length that has already passed ends the rest, a rest that ended while the field was open comes back if the new length has not passed, and the background countdown and end alert follow. Foreground completion shows an in-app signal, plays a short original chime and vibrates; background completion uses a local notification with the same chime and vibration. While a rest or interval phase runs with the app in the background, an ongoing silent notification counts it down; it clears when the app returns, the phase ends, or the timer is skipped, finished or discarded. On iOS this is a Live Activity on the Lock Screen and in the Dynamic Island; it starts with the timer, stays while the app is open, counts down the current phase only and shows **Rest timer up!** once that phase ends. Settings has **Sound** and **Haptics** switches, both on by default. Sound governs the chime, which also follows the phone's ringer: it stays silent while the ringer is on silent or vibrate. Haptics governs the rest-end vibration and every haptic in the app, except that on iOS a background notification vibrates only with its sound. All number entry, timers included, uses the in-app keypad in a bottom sheet. Durations are typed as hh:mm:ss, digits filling from the right, and shown as m:ss or h:mm:ss, never as a count of seconds.
- A template is a forward-facing, editable plan. A workout is an independent record, even when created from a template. A template may be made from a workout; a workout may be started from a template or earlier workout. Changing a template does not rewrite completed sessions.
- Templates can be placed on dates and repeated weekly. Moving, editing, or skipping one scheduled occurrence leaves the repeating plan intact. Planned-workout reminders are local, off by default, and arrive at a chosen time, 8:00 AM by default, on each planned day.
- Completed workouts remain editable: sets, exercise order, date, notes, and RPE can be corrected. Edits update dependent statistics. History is never silently rewritten by later template or catalog edits.

## Exercise catalog and muscles

- Bundle [Free Exercise DB](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db) metadata, instructions, equipment, muscle mapping, and appropriately sized local images. The dataset is [Unlicensed](https://github.com/yuhonas/free-exercise-db/blob/main/LICENSE.md); include a source notice with the packaged content. Validate fields and package size before bundling. No ExerciseDB API content: its [terms prohibit persistent storage](https://exercisedb.notion.site/ExerciseDB-API-Terms-of-Use-226983b728ca8090bf7be79564e4b356).
- Users create exercises with primary/secondary muscles and free-text equipment. A new exercise can start as a copy of any exercise, catalog or custom: its mode, equipment, muscles, rest, bar weight and note are copied, the name gains **(copy)**, and the user edits any of them before adding it. The copy has no How to. Copying from an exercise in a workout or template adds the copy right after that exercise (after its superset) and leaves the original in place; the user can remove it. A copied barbell, E-Z bar or trap bar exercise stays a bar exercise. Default and per-workout mapping edits are CLI-only; the app has no mapping editor. Each logged workout retains its own mapping snapshot, so later default changes affect only future sessions.
- Muscle sets count completed normal and drop sets in finished workouts; warm-ups and incomplete sets count zero. Each primary muscle receives 1 set, each secondary muscle 0.5. Both sides of a superset count through their own completed exercises. Count the 7 days ending today by recorded local workout date. Show the calculation rule next to the totals. Tapping a muscle's total lists the exercises that make it up, each with its sets by the same rule.
- Exercise history shows dated sets, best weight, best reps at a weight, and estimated one-rep max; a duration exercise shows its longest hold and the longest hold at each weight. Exclude warm-ups from records. Records come from completed working sets in finished workouts; ties keep the earliest set. Show the estimate formula and link each record to its source set. History also shows a rep-max table (≈ 3, 5, 8 and 10RM) from the best estimated one-rep max. A bodyweight lift's estimate adds the body weight logged on or before the workout to the added weight and is labelled **with body weight**; its weight records stay on the added weight.
- A **Stats** page, opened from **Show all** at the end of its home section, totals volume (weight × reps of completed working sets in finished workouts, adding the logged body weight on bodyweight lifts such as pull-ups, dips and push-ups) for the past week, past month, or all time, lists each lift's current best weight, estimated one-rep max and hold as PRs, most recently attained first, with any the user stars pinned first and shown on the home Stats section with a small trend line, and lists every lift with records, most recently trained first. A star follows a lift's record type, not one session, so each PR shows its gain since the first workout in its trend (or no change, or 1 workout) rather than the date it was set. A lift opens its own page: each of its records with its per-workout best over time (heaviest weight, estimated one-rep max or longest hold) and its exercise history; a starred PR on home opens the same page. The home past-week volume row opens a Volume page with the range totals and weekly volume over time, each week the 7 days ending on a date counting back from today. Completing a set that beats every earlier finished workout's heaviest weight, estimated one-rep max, or longest hold for that exercise marks it with a PR badge, animation, and haptic; reduced motion keeps the badge without animation.

## Measurements and photos

- Presets: body weight, neck, chest, waist, hips, and left/right bicep, forearm, thigh, and calf. Users may add a named custom measurement. Every entry has a date, value, and unit; each series has a trend chart. Users log several parts for one date in a single pass from the **+** in the Measurements header; a blank part logs nothing, and a tracked part's trend shows the typed value before saving. The number entry sheet never covers the field being edited.
- Each preset has a short visual guide for tape position, posture, and relaxed/flexed state. Keep the method consistent in that series and encourage similar measurement conditions. Limb presets are measured relaxed; a flexed measurement is a custom series. A custom series can store a method note.
- Progress photos attach to a date. **Log measurements** takes or picks them and copies each into app-private storage. They are never uploaded or saved to the public photo library; they leave the app only in the full-data export, which includes original files. Deleting a measurement or series keeps its photos.
- Measurement reminders are local, off by default, and arrive at a chosen time, 8:00 AM by default: daily, weekly on a weekday, fortnightly on a weekday, or monthly on a day of the month. A fortnight starts on the first chosen weekday on or after the day the reminder is turned on or its repeat or weekday changes. A monthly day past the end of a shorter month reminds on its last day. Times are typed on the keypad as 12-hour h:mm, digits filling from the right, with an AM/PM choice beside each time. Turning either reminder on asks for notification permission; when it is denied, Settings says so.
- Users star the series they most want to track; starred series are what other screens surface. **View all** shows progress by day: each row has the day's last photo and its starred values, or body weight when nothing is starred. Days spanning more than one calendar month group into month rows showing that month's last photo, expanding to the days.

## Ownership and portability

- Export one versioned ZIP with related CSV tables for exercises, templates, schedules, workouts, sets, measurements, and settings, plus a manifest and photo files, saved as a file in a folder the user picks. Each exercise's own note is the `note` column of `exercise.csv`. An older export without that column imports with local notes kept, even under **Replace**. Include an exportable blank CSV template with documented columns. Import also accepts the workout and measurement exports of other trackers (Strong, Hevy, FitNotes, StrengthLog and others as samples allow), in their metric or imperial units. Names are matched by a table of each app's catalog names, then by exact and similar name. The preview lists every exercise name in the file with the exercise it maps to; the user can change any, create it as a custom exercise, or skip it. A name with no exercise is skipped: its sets are left out, a workout left with no exercises is left out, and a superset left with one exercise is no longer a superset. Each name's exercise is remembered per app on the device and is not part of an export; a skip is not remembered. Importing the same file again adds only workouts that are not already here, so a skipped exercise in an imported workout stays out. Reading, checking and importing a large file show live progress, and the page cannot be left until the step finishes.
- Use CUIDs as stable row IDs. Imported rows with matching IDs, or matching unique keys such as a catalog exercise's source, are duplicates; rows from blank templates receive new IDs. Preview counts, validation errors, and conflicts before applying. Duplicate actions are **Skip**, **Update** populated imported fields, and **Replace** the complete row. A batch default may be changed per row. Import is all-or-nothing; never leave a partial restore. Import restores the photo files an export holds; a photo row imports only with its file.
- Settings offers **Erase all local data** behind clear confirmation and an export reminder. Normal encrypted OS device backup is permitted; manual export remains visible. An optional backup folder receives the full export after each finished workout; with no backup folder and no export in 30 days, home shows a dismissible reminder. There is one person's data store per installation.
- Health sync is off by default and lives in the **Data** group as a **Health Connect** (Android) or **Apple Health** (iOS) Off/On switch. Turning it on asks for write permission only and then writes every finished workout and body-weight entry. A finished workout is a strength-training session with its name, start and finish time; a workout logged for a past date with no duration, and an unfinished workout, are not written. A workout moved to another date keeps its clock times in its own time zone on the new date. Body weight is written in kilograms at noon of its date, or at the current time when that noon is still ahead. Each later edit rewrites that record and each delete removes it, whenever the app opens or returns to home and after each finish. Records keep the nicereps row ID, so edits replace rather than duplicate. With only one kind permitted, only that kind is written. Turning sync off, or erasing local data, leaves written records in the health store, which manages them from then on; turning it back on rewrites every record, replacing rather than duplicating any still there. A record the store rejects is reported under the switch and retried at the next sync without holding back the others. When the store is missing, needs an update or permission is off, Settings says so under the switch with a link to open or get it, and the rest of the app is unaffected. Below it, an Off/On **Read body weight** switch, off by default and independent of writing, asks for read permission for weight only. At each sync it adds body weight that other apps saved in the store to dates with no nicereps body-weight entry, taking the earliest sample of the day in the user's weight unit, rounded to 0.1; a date that already has a nicereps entry keeps it. Nothing else is read. Entries nicereps wrote are skipped, a read entry is never written back, and each store sample is considered once, so an entry deleted in nicereps stays deleted unless another sample is saved for that date; a nicereps entry equal to a sample on its date, such as one read before an export and import, counts as read. Erase forgets what was read and turns reading off. Editing a read entry keeps it out of the store. Turning reading off keeps the entries already added. Health Connect gives read access from 30 days before it was first granted; Apple Health does not report a denied read, so nothing is added and no problem is shown. Health sync state stays on the device and is not exported.

## Settings and quality

Settings has five headed groups in order: **App** (dark/light theme, dark default; home-section order by drag and visibility by a switch per section, Start staying first; haptics and sound), **Workout** (the bar and plates available for each weight unit, and planned-workout and measurement reminders), **Units** (kg/lb for weights, cm/in for tape measurements, km/mi for distances), **Data** and **About**. **About** holds **Rate nicereps** (the app's store listing, Android only until the App Store listing exists), **Report a bug** (an email to support@buildnice.app filled with the app version, OS version and, on Android, device model for the user to edit and send), **Source code**, a subtle Ko-fi link, and a note that the app never connects to the internet. These open the user's email, browser or store app; the app itself makes no request and never asks for a review unprompted. Unit changes convert display without losing stored precision. Initial app and catalog language is English.

Every screen supports screen readers, dynamic text, sufficient contrast, large touch targets, and reduced motion. The common workout path must remain usable with shaky hands and divided attention. Offline operation, app restart recovery, data round trip, local notifications, and both platform builds are first-release acceptance conditions.
